## 1. What do data sources say about winning creative formats in education specifically? (Motion, Meta success stories, WordStream/LocaliQ, agency reports)

### Inferences
- **Evidence hierarchy for education, strongest to weakest:**
  1. WordStream's vertical KPIs, which have no format detail.
  2. Meta's WhatsApp success stories, which cover destinations, not creative.
  3. Vendor CTRs by format (Think Orion) and organic engagement data (Emplifi).
  4. Audits of competitors' ads (VitalDesign, Domestika's ad mix).
  5. Agency cases that don't publish their creative.

  No source isolates the effect of a creative format on CPL or enrollment in a controlled way, so every ranking in section 2 rests on several directional signals that agree.
- **Read the WordStream 2026 numbers as a click problem, not a form problem.** Education lead campaigns convert well after the click (15.87% CVR, about 1.9x the all-industry median), while CTR is below average (1.74%). The bottleneck is stopping the scroll and earning the click. Superleads' design effort should go into hooks and first frames more than into the form. Caveat: the metrics are separate medians.
- **The VitalDesign audit points to a whitespace strategy.**
  - The gap: about 70% of grad-program ads use stock photos, only 16% use video, and only about 6% use testimonials (25% of the 24% that carry social proof).
  - The implication: real-student video testimonials are the clearest way to stand out.
  - The caveat: US grad programs are not Mexican universities. Before relying on this, browse the Meta Ad Library for each client's Mexican competitors. This is a recommended task, not a finding.

## 2. What do higher-ed and edtech marketers recommend, and how do the candidate formats rank by evidence?

### Takeaway
Practitioners agree, and partial data supports them: real students and instructors in native-looking vertical video beat polished brand or stock creative, and social proof is under-used. The best-supported formats to mock up first are:
1. the real-student testimonial Reel
2. the program carousel
3. the free class or micro-lesson ad

Quiz and info-session ads come next. Day-in-the-life (as a standalone format), campus tours, comparisons and career-outcome statics have no education performance data, so test them before investing.

**Format cards for the designers.** Beat timings, layouts and Spanish copy are my drafts unless a source is named.

**#1 Real-student / alumni testimonial Reel (9:16, 25–45 s, native look). Grade B.**
- **What it looks like:**
  - one real student or graduate talking to a phone camera, with captions burned in
  - one or two cuts of b-roll from the actual class, platform or campus
  - a chip on screen showing the RVOE (official SEP recognition) and the modality
  - an end card with the offer
- **Beats.** Built on the "before → turning point" structure ([Enrollify, Jan 2025](https://enrollify.org/episodes/ep-57-utilizing-video-in-higher-ed-marketing)):
  - 0–2 s: face plus an on-screen hook naming the program and the tension ("Terminé mi licenciatura trabajando de 9 a 6").
  - 2–10 s: before, the real constraint: time, money, fear of going back to school.
  - 10–22 s: turning point plus proof: how classes work (schedule, a screen recording of the platform, tutors) and official validity (RVOE/SEP).
  - 22–32 s: today, stated as fact (a promotion, a new job). No salary figures or guarantees (see section 4).
  - 32–40 s: offer and CTA ("Pide tu plan de estudios y costos por WhatsApp").
- **Why it works for education buyers:** enrolling is a high-risk decision with a long consideration period, and prospects pick schools where they can picture themselves ([Panopto](https://panopto.com/blog/video-in-higher-education-6-strategies-for-enrollment-success); opinion). Real students outperform stock ([VitalDesign law study, 2025](https://vitaldesign.com/university-meta-ads-study/)).
- **Evidence:**
  - Reels CTR 2.8% vs 1.4% for single images ([Think Orion, Apr 2026, vendor](https://thinkorion.com/blog/how-to-target-students-facebook-ads-ppc)).
  - Enrollment teams rank video ads among their most effective tactics, and institutions over-rely on polished brand content ([RNL 2025](https://www.ruffalonl.com/blog/enrollment/scrolling-for-success-how-social-media-shapes-college-planning-for-students)).
  - Competitor gap: 16% of grad-program ads use video, about 70% use stock and about 6% use testimonials ([VitalDesign](https://vitaldesign.com/university-meta-ad-campaign-study/)).
  - Native Reels style ([Edinburgh, 2023](https://case.org/awards/circle-excellence/2023/instagram-reel-questions-about-doing-online-masters); [CMU](https://cmich.edu/landing-pages/we-do-brand-campaign)).
- **Sub-vertical fit:**
  - High: licenciaturas ejecutivas and online degrees (a working adult on camera answers "no tengo tiempo"); prepa en línea for adults; undergraduate universities (also make a parent version); bootcamps (keep outcome claims compliant).
  - Medium-high: language schools.
  - Medium: K-12 (use a parent's testimonial, not a child's).
- **Variants:**
  - day-in-the-life: the same student in 6–8 s cuts across a day (work, evening class, weekend)
  - a student answering the top three FAQs
  - a professor version

**#2 Program carousel (1:1 or 4:5, 4–8 cards). Grade B.**
- **Layout:**
  - Card 1: the hook ("6 licenciaturas en línea con RVOE") plus the logo.
  - Middle cards: one program each, with name, modality, duration, one factual outcome line and a photo of a real student.
  - Last card: the offer and CTA ("Pide informes").
- **Variants:**
  - student stories (one student per card: quote, photo, program)
  - "lo que vas a aprender" modules (diplomados, bootcamps)
  - levels A1–C1 (language schools)
- **Evidence:**
  - Carousel CTR 2.1% vs 1.4% for single images ([Think Orion](https://thinkorion.com/blog/how-to-target-students-facebook-ads-ppc)).
  - Carousels and Reels lead organic Instagram engagement in education ([Emplifi, Q2 2026](https://emplifi.io/resources/q2-2026-education-worldwide-benchmarks/)).
  - Domestika's live mix leans on carousels: 67 of about 124 active ads ([AdMake](https://www.admakeai.com/advertisers/domestika)). This shows what a large edtech advertiser chooses, not performance.
  - Recommended by [Foreplay](https://www.foreplay.co/post/examples-of-facebook-ads-for-education).
- **Fit:** high for universities with many programs, online-course platforms and diplomados; medium for language schools and for K-12 (school levels and facilities).
- **Destination:** a Higher Intent instant form whose first question is the program (multi-select, see section 3), or a landing page with an anchor for each program.

**#3 Free class / micro-lesson / trial ad (video or animated static). Grade B.**
- **Video beats:**
  - 0–3 s: a teacher mid-lesson making a concrete micro-promise ("3 frases para presentarte en inglés en una entrevista").
  - 3–20 s: teach one thing.
  - 20–30 s: "Esto es una probadita de la clase muestra gratis del jueves 7 pm".
  - CTA: "Aparta tu lugar".
- **Animated static:**
  - a real photo of the class or teacher with a light motion loop (animated beat static for Soleil Provence)
  - headline "CLASE MUESTRA GRATIS", plus date, time and modality
  - "Cupo por grupo: [N]" only if the cap is real
  - a claim-style button ("Aparta tu lugar"); in one A/B test "Claim" beat "Sign Up"
- **Evidence** (all vendor-reported, but consistent):
  - **Linda Mandarin:** "Claim" beat "Sign Up" and lifted trial signups 20%; the case title claims CPA nearly halved ([Equinet, undated](https://www.equinetacademy.com/?p=56991)).
  - **Soleil Provence:** trial-lesson lead ads offering a 20% discount to students who attend a trial ([Humble Bunny](https://www.humblebunny.com/work/soleil-provence-b2c-lead-generation-case-study/)).
  - **English school (Spilno, Google Ads):** a free trial lesson as the lead magnet; cost per trial booking fell 45% ([Spilno](https://spilnoagency.com.ua/en/english-school-marketing)).
  - **Online IT school:** a free webinar as the first step took CPL from $48 to $18 ([Freelancehunt](https://freelancehunt.com/en/showcase/work/meta-ads-case-online-school/2036380.html)).
  - **Staged offers:** better than pushing "Apply Now" early ([VitalDesign law study](https://vitaldesign.com/university-meta-ads-study/)).
- **Fit:**
  - High: language schools; bootcamps (as a free workshop); online courses and diplomados.
  - Medium: universities ("clase muestra de Psicología"); K-12 ("clase abierta para papás").
- **Destination:** an instant form with a session-slot question, or a CTWA booking. Send WhatsApp reminders, because show rates are a risk (see #5).

**#4 Quiz entry ad: vocational test or level test. Grade B− (only one quantified quiz case, undated).**
- **Static:**
  - vocational: "¿Qué carrera va contigo? Test vocacional gratis · resultado en 3 min", four icons for career areas, CTA "Hacer el test"
  - level test: "¿Cuál es tu nivel real de inglés? Examen de ubicación gratis en 10 min"
- **Video:** a student takes the quiz on a phone, then the result screen is revealed.
- **Evidence:**
  - **Unity College:** a career quiz built in Qzzr covering its 16 environmental majors, promoted with Facebook ads to three personas. Leads tripled on the same budget, web traffic rose 33% and CPL fell 11%. The best-performing message had a direct CTA to take the quiz ([Media Cause, undated](https://mediacause.com/our-work/case-study/persona-marketing-triples-qualified-leads/)).
  - **VIA University College:** a quiz leading to a free e-guide on choosing a career ([dot.vu](https://dot.vu/customer-examples/do-you-know-the-career-type)).
  - **Zapier's example:** the AI-readiness quiz static ([Zapier](https://zapier.com/blog/facebook-ad-examples/)).
  - **Online IT school:** added a qualification quiz to its form ([Freelancehunt](https://freelancehunt.com/en/showcase/work/meta-ads-case-online-school/2036380.html)).
  - No case with a level test as the lead magnet was found.
- **Fit:**
  - High for vocational tests: prepa students and parents, undergraduate admissions.
  - Plausible but untested for level tests: language schools.
  - Bootcamps: "¿Qué ruta tech es para ti?".
- **Destination:** a quiz landing page that captures the lead before showing the result. For very short quizzes, an instant form with conditional logic. Teen targeting is limited (see section 4).

**#5 Info session / open house / webinar event ad. Grade C.**
- **Static:**
  - an event card: "Sesión informativa en línea · jue 7:00 pm · 40 min · planes, costos y becas", with a photo of the real coordinator who hosts it and a "Regístrate" button
  - K-12 version: "Open house sábado 10 am · conoce el colegio"
- **Evidence:**
  - **RapidDev:** lowest cost per webinar registration $8.06, with 370 registrations and 48 sales-qualified leads, for a US business-education company ([RapidDev](https://www.rapidevelopers.com/case-study/meta-ads-lead-generation-webinar-registration-case-study)).
  - **Online IT school:** a free webinar as the first step ([Freelancehunt](https://freelancehunt.com/en/showcase/work/meta-ads-case-online-school/2036380.html)).
  - **British Council education fair:** only 50% of registrants showed up ([British Council](https://opportunities-insight.britishcouncil.org/download/data/26734/10816)).
  - **National Academy of Sciences open house:** $1.65 per ticket reserved through social ads ([Social Driver](https://www.socialdriver.com/case-studies/nas)). [OLD 2019, not education]
  - `lead_gen_servicios.md` cites Jeremy Haynes on lower show rates for weekly webinars.
- **Fit:** high for posgrado, online degrees, licenciaturas ejecutivas, K-12 open houses and bootcamps.
- **Destination:** an instant form with a date question, plus WhatsApp confirmations and reminders. Measure cost per attendee, not cost per registration.

**#6 Offer-first static (beca, inscripción, MSI, deadline). Grade C for education.**
- **Layout** follows `formatos_creativos.md` archetype A1:
  - Top third: the offer in 6 words or fewer ("Beca de hasta 40%").

- **Evidence:**

## 3. Which offers, lead magnets, destinations and qualifying questions work best in education?

### Takeaway
Across vendor cases, low-commitment first steps work: a free trial class or workshop, a quiz, a webinar or info session, or free educational resources. Pushing "Apply now" early is common and is criticized. Instant forms give the cheapest leads but need required qualifying questions to protect lead quality. In LatAm, three education cases published by Meta favor click-to-WhatsApp (CTWA), with conversion and enrollment gains, and Meta's AI Business Agent now runs inside WhatsApp in Mexico.

### Cited Findings

#### Offers and lead magnets
- **Trial class:**
  - Linda Mandarin: +20% trial signups after a "Claim" vs "Sign Up" CTA test in a retargeting campaign aimed at site visitors who hadn't booked a trial ([Equinet Academy](https://www.equinetacademy.com/?p=56991)).
  - Soleil Provence: Meta lead ads for a trial lesson, with a 20% discount for students who attend it plus urgency language ([Humble Bunny](https://www.humblebunny.com/work/soleil-provence-b2c-lead-generation-case-study/)).
  - An English school (Google Ads): a free trial lesson as the magnet, with bookings tracked rather than raw leads; cost per trial booking fell 45% in two months ([Spilno](https://spilnoagency.com.ua/en/english-school-marketing)).

**Offer ladder for Mexico** (first step → second step), my synthesis:
- **Universities:** test vocacional or plan de estudios + costos → info session or campus visit → solicitud.
- **Licenciaturas ejecutivas and online degrees:** plan de estudios + costos by WhatsApp → evening info session → beca or MSI offer with a deadline.

- **K-12:** open house or guided visit → enrollment discount or sibling discount.

**Where to send the click:**

| Situation | Destination | Why (cited above) |
|---|---|---|
| Volume at low CPL, simple offer (plan de estudios, beca, info session) | Higher Intent instant form + 3–4 required questions | WordStream CVR; DOM: required questions improve matriculation |
| Adults in MX who want to ask about price or schedule (licenciaturas ejecutivas, prepa abierta, language schools) | CTWA, with an AI agent or advisor answering within minutes | Universidad Uk 2x; Politécnico 4x leads / 2.1x enrollments; Meta Business Agent in MX |
| Quiz, test, or a program comparison that needs explaining | Landing page (quiz) | Unity College; Colling Media on quality from website conversions |
| Events (info session, open house, webinar) | Instant form with a date question + WhatsApp reminders | 50% show rate at the British Council fair |

**Instant-form template in Spanish.** Items 1, 2 and 6 rest on the sources above; items 3–5 and 7 are my additions:
1. ¿Qué programa te interesa? (multi-select, mapped to the catalog)
2. ¿Cuándo te gustaría iniciar? (próximo inicio [mes] / este año / el próximo año / solo estoy explorando)
3. Modalidad (en línea / presencial / mixta)
4. Último nivel de estudios terminado (secundaria / prepa / licenciatura). Needed to know whether the lead qualifies for prepa or licenciatura. Ask it as a neutral question; never put it in ad copy as "you + attribute" (section 4).
5. ¿Para quién es la información? (para mí / para mi hijo o hija). For prepa and K-12.
6. Plantel or ciudad (campus-based programs only)
7. Horario preferido para contactarte + WhatsApp number

Make 1, 2 and 4 required, following the DOM result. Track cost per qualified lead and lead-to-enrollment rate (3–5% reference, HeyFlow), not raw CPL.

## 4. Which Meta policies shape education ads?

### Takeaway
Four Meta rules matter most:
- **Personal attributes:** ads may not assert or imply a viewer's attributes, and age is on the list, so "¿Tienes más de 25 y…?" is risky.
- **Unrealistic outcomes:** bans guaranteed jobs or income without specifics, which governs bootcamp and career copy.
- **Teen targeting:** limited to age and location since 2023.
- **Special ad categories:** professional certification and training programs count as employment ads, but self-declaring the category is only required for US advertisers or ads targeting the US, Canada or parts of Europe.

### Cited Findings
- **Personal attributes (Meta, official):**
  - Ads must not contain content that asserts or implies personal attributes, including indirect implications.
  - The list runs from race and religion to health, financial status, criminal record and name.
  - Meta says such ads can feel intrusive, unsettling or inaccurate.

  — [Meta Transparency Center, Privacy violations and personal attributes](https://transparency.meta.com/policies/ad-standards/objectionable-content/privacy-violations-personal-attributes/)
- **Age is a protected attribute,** according to a third-party summary of the list (race, religion, age, disability, health condition, financial status, criminal record, name). — [Stackmatix](https://www.stackmatix.com/blog/meta-ads-personal-attributes-policy)
- **The usual trap is "you"/"your"** when it implies the advertiser knows the reader has one of these attributes. [PRACTITIONER] — [Jon Loomer](https://www.jonloomer.com/can-we-use-the-words-you-and-your-in-facebook-ads-copy/)
- **No education examples:** none of the sources gave a Meta example about education copy ("high school", "degree"). — (search log)
- **Unrealistic outcomes (Meta, official)** prohibits:
  - "Guarantees or promises of employment, financial gain, or other benefits without information on the specific job or title, responsibilities, requirements, or risks"
  - claims that a person can become rich quickly
  - claims that little or no work is needed to make money

  Ads for income opportunities must fully describe the business model. — [Meta, Unrealistic outcomes](https://transparency.meta.com/policies/ad-standards/deceptive-content/unrealistic-outcomes)
- **Review looks beyond the ad:** income claims are reviewed as misleading claims, and reviewers look at primary text, headlines, images, landing pages and lead forms together. The extract didn't say which page made which point. [VENDOR, 2026] — [Enrich Labs](https://www.enrichlabs.ai/blog/meta-ads-policy-guide-2026); [Stackmatix](https://www.stackmatix.com/blog/meta-ads-income-claims-policy)
- **Who must declare a special ad category (Meta, official):** "any United States advertiser or advertiser targeting the United States, Canada or certain parts of Europe that is running financial products and services, housing or employment ads, must self identify as a Special Ad Category." — [Meta, Introduction to the Advertising Standards](https://transparency.meta.com/policies/ad-standards/)
- **The current categories** are housing, employment, financial products and services, and issues/elections/politics. Ads may be rejected if the advertiser doesn't choose the right category. — [Meta Marketing API docs (2026)](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/ads-commerce/marketing-api/audiences/special-ad-category)
- **What counts as employment** (third-party summaries; not confirmed in Meta's own text):
  - Covered: full-time and part-time jobs, internships, apprenticeships, professional certification programs, job fairs, job boards. Sources: [Data-Axle (2025)](https://www.data-axle.com/resources/blog/meta-special-ad-categories-rules/); [ConductAtlas](https://conductatlas.com/platform/meta/meta-special-ad-category-requirements/employment-ads-special-ad-category/); [Plai](https://help.plai.io/article/setting-up-special-ads-categories-credit-employment-housing); [Jon Loomer](https://www.jonloomer.com/special-ad-categories-meta-ads/).
  - Not covered: general career advice that isn't tied to job listings. Source: [Data-Axle (2025)](https://www.data-axle.com/resources/blog/meta-special-ad-categories-rules/).
  - Targeting limits in the category: no targeting or exclusion by age, gender or ZIP code, and limited detailed targeting. Sources: [ConductAtlas](https://conductatlas.com/platform/meta/meta-special-ad-category-requirements/employment-ads-special-ad-category/); [Data-Axle (2025)](https://www.data-axle.com/resources/blog/meta-special-ad-categories-rules/).
- **Agile-ed's claim:** campaigns in special ad categories "may see frequent rejections despite following all guidelines". [VENDOR MARKETING] — [agile-ed](https://agile-ed.com/?p=16597)
- **Teens (announced January 2023):**
  - Advertisers can target under-18s globally by age and location only.
  - Gender is no longer available, and in-app activity (accounts followed, Pages liked) is not used.
  - Teens got "See Less" controls for ad topics.

  — [Search Engine Land](https://searchengineland.com/meta-introduces-new-ad-targeting-limits-for-teens-391259); [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/Meta-Expands-Ad-Targeting-Restrictions-for-Teens/640082/); [Marketing Dive](https://www.marketingdive.com/news/meta-adds-additional-ad-targeting-restrictions-for-teen-users/640287/)

  - Education is not a special ad category.
  - Special ad categories don't apply to Mexico-only targeting.
  - The Ley Federal de Protección al Consumidor (LFPC) covers price, scholarship and "inscripción gratis" promotions.
  - The obligation to show RVOE in advertising is unverified background knowledge.

### Inferences
**Compliant rewrites for education copy** (my drafts; test them in Ads Manager):

| Risky (asserts an attribute or outcome) | Safer (describes the program) |
|---|---|
| "¿No terminaste la prepa?" | "Prepa en línea para adultos, con validez oficial SEP" |
| "¿Tienes más de 25 años y no tienes título?" | "Licenciatura ejecutiva en línea: clases por la noche y en fin de semana" |
| "Gana $40,000 al mes como programador" | "Bootcamp de desarrollo web de 24 semanas: proyectos reales y bolsa de trabajo. No garantizamos empleo." |
| "Consigue trabajo garantizado al terminar" | "8 de cada 10 egresados trabajan en su área (encuesta de egresados 2025, n = [N])", only with real, documented data |
| "¿Tu hijo va mal en matemáticas?" | "Regularización en matemáticas para secundaria en [colonia]" |

- **Education level vs age.** Education level isn't explicitly listed in the excerpts I saw, but the "you + attribute" construction is the common trigger, and age *is* on the list. Rewrite both the same way.
- **Prepa and K-12 audiences.**
  - Teen campaigns (15–17) can target only age and location. The creative has to do the self-selection: name the program, the plantel and the ciudad.
  - Run a separate campaign for parents (adults, full targeting).
  - K-12 should be aimed at parents.
- **Bootcamps and certifications aimed only at Mexico** don't need to declare the employment category, under Meta's standards text.
  - They do if the advertiser is US-based or the ads target the US, e.g., the Hispanic market.
  - Keep wording neutral and factual anyway, because automated review may still flag training ads (agile-ed's claim is unverified).
- **The landing page and form are reviewed with the ad.** Salary or "guarantee" claims on the landing page can get the ad rejected even if the ad copy is clean.

### Gaps
- **Meta's own worked examples** for personal attributes weren't retrieved. Whether education level (or not finishing school) counts as an attribute in Meta's internal enforcement is unconfirmed.
- **Children in ads:** Meta's rules on showing minors in ad creative, and Mexican rules on using minors' images (LGDNNA) and their data (LFPDPPP consent for quizzes and forms filled in by teens), were not researched.
- **Mexican sector rules (both unverified, not researched):**
  - whether private schools must state their RVOE in advertising
  - PROFECO rules on scholarship promotions
- **Health careers:** whether health-career programs (e.g., nursing) trigger Meta's health and wellness restrictions was not checked.
- **Employment special ad category:** Meta's primary text on its scope (including professional certification) was not retrieved; the scope comes from third parties.

## 5. Benchmarks: education CPL, CTR and CVR on Meta (US, LatAm/Mexico)

### Takeaway
The only large, neutral education benchmark is WordStream's US data:
- **2026:** CPL $26.31, CVR 15.87%, lead CTR 1.74%, CPC $1.72
- **2025:** CPL $28.22, CVR 10.08%, CTR 1.86%, CPC $1.65

### Inferences
- **Benchmark against conversion, not clicks.** In the US, education is close to the all-industry CPL but converts nearly twice as well after the click. Judge Superleads' education campaigns on CVR and cost per qualified lead or enrollment, not on CTR or CPC alone.
- **Don't convert US dollar benchmarks into Mexican targets.** The MX figures are thin (agency blogs, mixed definitions). The most reliable Mexican benchmark will be Superleads' own client data, split by sub-vertical (university, licenciatura ejecutiva, prepa, language school, diplomado, bootcamp, K-12) and by destination (instant form vs CTWA).
- **The case-level spread ($6–$160 per lead, before even counting enrollments)** shows that offer type and audience (parents vs adults vs teens) drive cost more than the vertical average does. Benchmark each offer separately: clase muestra, info session, plan de estudios, test.

### Gaps
- No Mexico-specific education CPL, CTR or CVR from a primary benchmark (WordStream-style, Meta, or a large Mexican agency dataset) was found. SuperAds' Mexico page didn't surface.
- WordStream's education row for traffic campaigns in 2026, and its all-industry lead CTR and CPC, weren't in the extracts.
- No benchmark splits education by sub-vertical (K-12 vs higher ed vs language vs bootcamp) or by destination (instant form vs CTWA vs landing page).
- Emplifi's paid CPC and CPM for education are unverified (conflicting extracts).
