## 1. Which creative archetypes are most cited as winners in 2025–2026 (data sources, top US strategists, brands)?

### Takeaway
Motion's Creative Benchmarks 2026 is the only large, recent dataset I found: $1.29B of Meta spend, about 579K creatives and about 6K accounts, covering ads launched Sep 2025–Jan 2026. By Motion's spend-based "hit rate", the strongest visual formats are:
- Stitch (12.5%) and Reaction video (11.2%).
- Unboxing (9.8%).
- Offer-first banner (8.6%). It also takes the largest share of spend, 29.3%.
- Founder (8.6%) and Demo (8.1%).
- Testimonial (6.6%) and Us-vs-them (6.5%) sit in the middle, and Listicle (5.3%) is last.

Cheap-to-make asset types (text-only, product image + text, UGC) win more often than high-production content. The named strategists (Barry Hott, Savannah Sanchez, Dara Denney, Sarah Levinger) don't back one "magic format". They agree on native-looking, lo-fi execution, real people on camera, and starting from the angle or persona instead of the format. Several popular candidates had no 2025–26 performance data in anything I could retrieve: Reddit and Google-search screenshots, POV, ASMR, myth-vs-fact, split-screen and day-in-the-life.

### Cited Findings

**Motion Creative Benchmarks 2026: dataset and definitions [DATA; vendor-published, Motion sells the AI tagging that produces it]**
- Dataset: $1.29B in Meta ad spend, 578,750 creatives and 6,015 advertiser accounts. Some coverage rounds this to about 550K ads and $1.3B. The creatives were launched Sep 1, 2025 – Jan 1, 2026, and are split into 5 spend tiers and 15 verticals. Sources: [Motion, Creative Benchmarks 2026](https://motionapp.com/library/research/creative-benchmarks-2026/); [Motion methodology](https://motionapp.com/library/research/creative-benchmarks-2026/methodology); [TipRanks launch coverage](https://www.tipranks.com/news/private-companies/motion-launches-2026-creative-benchmark-report-based-on-1-3-billion-in-ad-spend)
- A "winner" is a creative that spends at least 10× the account's median single-ad spend (one description adds a $500 floor). About 5% of ads qualify, and winners take about 55% of spend. Roughly half of all ads never get meaningful spend. Sources: [Motion](https://motionapp.com/library/research/creative-benchmarks-2026/); [Motion, spend concentration](https://motionapp.com/library/research/creative-benchmarks-2026/spend-concentration)
- The share of spend going to winners rises with advertiser size: 23% for Micro accounts, 64% for Enterprise. Summaries put the winner rate at about 4% for the smallest accounts and about 8–9% for enterprise. Sources: [Motion, spend concentration](https://motionapp.com/library/research/creative-benchmarks-2026/spend-concentration); [Foxwell Digital summary](https://www.foxwelldigital.com/blog/motion-creative-benchmarks-2026-8-key-takeaways)
- Weekly creative output grows with spend: about 2.8 new creatives a week for accounts under $10K, up to 18.85 a week for accounts over $1M. Top-quartile accounts launch 2–3× more creative than the average account in the same spend tier. These figures come from secondary summaries of Motion. Sources: [Sepia Lab](https://sepia-lab.com/en/blog/ad-creative-volume-benchmarks); [Taylor Sicard](https://taylorsicard.com/blog/meta-ads-creative-benchmarks-2026)
- "Spend use ratio" (SUR) is a format's share of spend divided by its share of creatives. Above 1.0 means the format gets more spend than its volume would predict. Source: [Motion, top visual formats](https://motionapp.com/library/research/creative-benchmarks-2026/top-visual-formats)
- Caveats from Motion and its coverage:
  - The findings are associations, not causation.
  - Outcomes are not tied to ROAS, revenue or conversions. A "winner" is simply an ad Meta chose to spend on.
  - In Motion's words, "hit rate reflects how often rare events occur within an account, not how 'good' a team's ideas are".
  - Leaderboards only include segments with at least 50 accounts.
  - Results are time-bound, and Black Friday/Cyber Monday (BFCM) effects are present in the data.

  Sources: [Motion](https://motionapp.com/library/research/creative-benchmarks-2026/); [Motion, top asset types](https://motionapp.com/library/research/creative-benchmarks-2026/top-asset-types)

## 2. Replicable templates per archetype: layout and beat structure, copy patterns, production level, placements, failure modes, and fit for ecommerce vs lead gen vs local services vs B2B

### Takeaway
The sources explain how each archetype works and how it fails, but none publishes tested beat-by-beat timings, so the template cards below are my own synthesized blueprints. The sourced constraints that matter most:
- Native and screenshot formats work because they don't look like ads. Fake testimonials, impersonation and misleading UI break Meta's ad standards and FTC rules.
- Before/after is restricted in health and beauty. Third-party guides say the rules were loosened in July 2026, but I couldn't verify that.
- Meta's Personal Attributes policy bans "you"-framing about health, body or finances. This hits Superleads' health/aesthetics and finance clients hardest.

### Cited Findings

**Native screenshot family (Notes app, iMessage/text thread, X post)**
- How it works, according to commentators:
  - Native-style ads avoid the instant "this is sponsored" dismissal.
  - Text threads borrow credibility from private messaging, "a context... never colonized by advertising".
  - A Notes or text screenshot passes for a friend's screenshot, which buys an extra beat of attention.

- Evidence quality: the only controlled test I found is a NextAfter / Buckner International experiment with a Notes-app-style Facebook ad, from around 2024 [OLD]. Its results weren't visible in the extract. Claims such as "fake text beats studio video by 34% on CTR" come from unnamed buyers [UNVERIFIED]. Sources: [NextAfter](https://www.nextafter.com/experiments/how-using-an-iphone-notes-app-style-of-facebook-ad-creative-impact-clicks/); [Influencers-Time, iMessage mockups](https://www.influencers-time.com/fake-text-ads-why-imessage-mockups-beat-studio-video/)

**Advertorial / native article (ad plus pre-lander)**
- Match the landing page to the buyer's awareness: advertorials or quizzes for unaware and problem-aware buyers, listicles for solution-aware buyers, product pages for retargeting and product-aware buyers. Judge by cost per purchase and revenue per session, not CTR. Source: [Succession Media (supplement guide, 2026)](https://succession.media/guides/meta-ads-landing-page-vs-product-page-supplements/)
- A page-builder vendor cites a CorePPC benchmark: dedicated pre-sell pages convert 2–3× better than product pages, against a product-page baseline of 1.8–2.5% [VENDOR]. Source: [Landra statistics](https://www.getlandra.com/blog/advertorial-listicle-conversion-statistics)
- Fake testimonials, stolen photos and invented claims break Meta's ad standards and the FTC's native-advertising rules, whatever the format. †Sources: [Landra native ad examples](https://www.getlandra.com/blog/native-ad-examples); [AdLibrary](https://adlibrary.com/guides/native-image-ads-guide)
- I found no independent 2025–26 study comparing advertorial and product-page performance on Meta. That gap is the search finding itself.

**Founder-led / talking head**
- Agency blogs make these claims [VENDOR/agency]:
  - Founder-to-camera ads often beat high-production brand creative because they "feel more believable".
  - One agency says founder-led ads get 28% lower CPAs than polished brand video across its clients.
  - Another says the talking head "usually wins on lead quality even when its CPL is a little higher".
  - Keep them 30–60 seconds, casual, and avoid the hard sell.
  - Rehearsed delivery is "the single most common failure mode".

**Us vs them**

  - A small checkmark grid heads off "but does it do X?".

### Inferences
**How to read these cards:** each card is a blueprint I put together from the mechanisms cited above plus standard direct-response practice. The timings and layouts are starting points to test, not measured findings. The fit ratings (High/Med/Low) are my judgment.

Default specs for every card follow Q5:
- Make a 9:16 master and keep text and logos inside the central safe area: out of the top 14%, the bottom 35% and 6% on each side.
- Export a 4:5 version for Feed.
- Burn in captions.
- Make the hook land in the first 1–2 seconds.

**A. Archetypes with data behind them**

1. **Offer-first banner (offer-led static).** Evidence: Motion, 8.6% HR and 29.3% of spend.
   - Looks like: a static, or a 3–6 s animated static, where the headline *is* the offer.
   - Layout: top third, the offer in 6 words or fewer with a big number; middle, the hero visual (product, result, a smiling client, a property facade); bottom third, a proof chip (★ rating · number of clients), the deadline and a CTA button graphic.
   - Copy pattern (EN): "[Offer] for [who] — ends [date]" / "Get [result] for [price]". MX lead-gen (ES): "Valoración sin costo + 20% en tu primer tratamiento — solo en octubre".
   - Production: low (Figma or Canva). Meta's 2026 text-in-image generation can produce variants.
   - Fit: Ecom High; Local services High; Education High (enrollment deadlines, scholarships); Real estate Med-High (pre-sale price, down-payment plans); B2B Med (a free audit or diagnostic as the "offer").
   - Fails when: the offer is weak or never ends; discounts attract low-quality leads; regulated pricing or financing claims lack disclaimers; Q4 seasonality inflates results.

2. **Stitch / reaction (an expert reacts to a clip or claim).** Evidence: Motion's top HR (12.5% / 11.2%), and top in Health & Wellness.
   - Beats:
     - 0–3 s: the source clip or an on-screen claim (a myth, a viral tip, a customer question), plus "Wait—".
     - 3–5 s: cut to the person reacting, in split screen or picture-in-picture.
     - 5–25 s: the correction or explanation, with visual proof.
     - 25–35 s: CTA.
   - Copy: "Stop doing this if you want [result]" / "Reacting to the worst [category] advice on the internet". MX (ES): "Dentista reacciona: ¿de verdad funciona blanquear con bicarbonato?"
   - Production: lo-fi, phone plus an editing app. Use your own source clips or licensed ones.
   - Fit: Health/aesthetics High; Professional services and finance High (credibility plus explanation); Education High; Local services Med; Ecom High.
   - Fails when: the reaction is low-energy, the source clip has nothing to do with the offer, or the claim being corrected is a straw man.

3. **Unboxing / "what you get" reveal.** Evidence: Motion, 9.8% HR on only 2.1% of volume, so it is under-used.
   - Beats:
     - 0–2 s: box or kit in hand, with text such as "Everything inside a $___ ___".
     - 2–10 s: close-ups of each item.
     - 10–20 s: first use or first impression.
     - 20–30 s: offer and CTA.
   - Lead-gen version: reveal the deliverables. Examples: a course's welcome kit and materials, the inspection report a service hands over, a walkthrough of a model apartment, "what's included in your treatment package", a B2B onboarding kit or dashboard.
   - Fit: Ecom High; Education Med-High; Real estate Med; Local services Med; B2B Low-Med.
   - Fails when: the deliverable is intangible and there's nothing visual to show.

4. **Founder / owner / doctor talking head.** Evidence: Motion, 8.6% HR; agency claims on CPA and lead quality.
   - Beats:
     - 0–3 s: a pattern-break line plus who you are ("I own a [business], and here's what most [category] won't tell you").
     - 3–15 s: the origin story or the problem.
     - 15–35 s: what we do differently, plus one piece of proof.
     - 35–45 s: offer and CTA.
   - Copy: the hooks match Motion's tactics, such as confession ("I almost closed my clinic in 2023…"), bold claim and contrast. MX (ES): "Soy la Dra. ___ y esto es lo que nadie te explica antes de un ___".
   - Production: phone, a natural setting (clinic, office, job site), a lav mic and burned-in captions.
   - Fit: Local services High; Health/aesthetics High; Professional services and B2B High; Education High; Ecom Med-High (not fashion).
   - Fails when: the delivery sounds rehearsed, the founder is uncomfortable on camera, or the intro runs long before any value.

5. **Demo / process ("watch how it works").** Evidence: Motion, 8.1% HR; demonstration and process win in home/lifestyle.
   - Beats:
     - 0–2 s: the result first, or the problem happening.
     - 2–15 s: the process step by step.
     - 15–25 s: the outcome and proof.
     - Then the CTA.
   - Lead-gen version: screen-recorded software demos (B2B), "what happens in your first consultation", time-lapses of an installation or repair (local services), virtual property tours (real estate).
   - Fit: High for every segment.
   - Fails when: the steps are slow or the result can't be seen.

6. **Customer testimonial / UGC review (single or multi-UGC).** Evidence: Motion, 6.6% HR and about 13% of volume and spend; UGC asset type near the top; Sanchez's multi-UGC tip.
   - Beats:
     - 0–3 s: a result-first quote in the customer's own words.
     - 3–12 s: how things were before.
     - 12–30 s: the experience and one specific result.
     - Then the CTA.
     - Multi-UGC version: 3–5 customers, one line each.
   - Static version: a "golden nugget" review (Denney's term; my reading is that it pulls the single most persuasive line from a real review). Show that line huge, with 5 stars and a name or photo.
   - Lead-gen version: real client testimonials with signed consent, or Google-review screenshots for local businesses.
   - Fit: High for every segment.
   - Fails when: the praise is generic, the delivery sounds scripted, or any review is invented or edited. That is prohibited.

7. **Us vs them.** Evidence: Motion, 6.5% HR (mid-pack).
   - Static layout: a headline on top ("Why [city] families switch to ___"). Below it, two columns: the brand side in color with ✓, and the "typical [category]" side in gray with ✗. Then 4–6 rows of buyer criteria, with the CTA at the bottom. The video version uses split screen with alternating beats.
   - MX (ES): "Nosotros vs. la clínica típica: precio cerrado ✓ / cargos sorpresa ✗".
   - Fit: Ecom High; Local services High; Education High (vs self-study or YouTube); B2B High (vs a freelancer or in-house team); Real estate Med (buying direct from the developer vs through a broker).
   - Fails when: the differentiators are weak or generic, a competitor is named (disapproval and legal risk), or the claims can't be backed up.

8. **Listicle / "3 reasons why".** Evidence: Motion, 5.3% HR, the lowest of the named formats.
   - Static: a numbered list over a hero image. Carousel: one reason per card. Video: numbered beats of 3–5 s each.
   - Use it to add variety, not as a core bet. It is strongest when combined with mistakes-to-avoid or myth framing.
   - MX (ES): "3 errores antes de comprar en preventa".

9. **Celebrity / creator / partnership ad.** Evidence: Celebrity has 2.1× SUR on 0.8% of volume; partnership-ad lift data is in Q5.
   - Lead-gen version: local micro-influencers, athletes or respected local professionals, run as partnership ads from their own handles.
   - Fails when: there's no believable link between the person and the service, or rights and disclosure are unclear.

10. **Letter / Post-it / unconventional text placement statics.** Evidence: 1.7× / 1.3× / 1.5× SUR; Post-it is top in fashion.
    - Looks like: a handwritten letter from the owner or director ("Dear future patient…"); a sticky note on a mirror, fridge or desk; text placed on objects in the scene.
    - Lead-gen version: a director's letter to parents (education), a note from the doctor, a handwritten quote card from a realtor.
    - Production: very low.

11. **Quiz / meme / stylized product shot.** Evidence: these lead the Fashion leaderboard.
    - A quiz feeding into a lead form is a natural lead-gen bridge ("Which treatment fits your skin? Take the 30-second quiz").
    - Memes need culturally fluent, Mexico-specific references.

12. **Transformation / before-after.** Evidence: Motion (Health & Wellness); restricted by policy.
    - Layout: a 50/50 split or slider labeled "Antes / Después", with the timeframe and a disclaimer. Video: before at 0–3 s, the process at 3–15 s, then the after reveal.
    - Fit: home services High (remodeling, cleaning, landscaping); real-estate staging High; education skill progression Med; aesthetics and weight loss **only within Meta's current policy** (18+, no statements of inferiority, possibly no anti-aging before/after).
    - Fails when: it's rejected for policy, the results look implausible, or it uses personal-attribute "you" framing.

**B. Archetypes endorsed by practitioners (no hard 2025–26 data)**

13. **Ugly / lo-fi / news-style (Hott).** A phone photo or plain text on white, MS-Paint arrows, meme templates, and minimal branding with no logo up front.
    - Fit: everywhere, especially B2B and local services, where polish signals "this is an ad".
    - Fails when: "ugly" turns into unclear. The message and the offer still need to be obvious.

14. **Native screenshot family.**
    - Notes app: "Things I wish I knew before ___", then 5–7 lines, with the brand in the last line.
    - Text thread: 4–8 bubbles. A friend asks, a friend recommends, and the last bubble shows the result.
    - X post: avatar, handle and a 1–3-line hot take.
    - In Mexico, the native equivalent of iMessage is a WhatsApp chat screenshot. That's my inference; see the separate MX/WhatsApp research.
    - Fails when: it impersonates real people or brands, presents an invented review or conversation as genuine, or mimics system notifications. Avoid all three; use content that is clearly brand-written or that you have consent for.

15. **Green screen.** The creator stands in the foreground, and the background is a screenshot: Google reviews, a news headline about a regulation, a listing, a course syllabus or a price table. The creator points at it and explains. Sanchez calls this core for app and service ads, so it fits lead gen well.

16. **Street interview.**
    - Beats:
      - 0–2 s: the question on screen, with the mic in frame.
      - 2–20 s: 3–6 quick answers.
      - 20–30 s: the reveal, a try-out or an explanation, plus the CTA.
    - MX (ES): "¿Cuánto crees que cuesta un departamento en ___?"
    - Fit: Real estate, finance and education High; local services Med.
    - Fails when: it's obviously staged (paid actors), it doesn't connect to the product, or releases are missing.

17. **Podcast clip.** Start mid-conversation on a bold statement, add a lower-third with name and credential, and always caption it. Fit: High for B2B, professional services, real-estate market talk and health expert interviews.

18. **Advertorial plus pre-lander.** The ad is framed as an editorial headline ("Why [city] homeowners are switching to ___") and links to an article-style page that runs story, proof, offer, then a form or WhatsApp button. Never imply that a real publication endorses it or wrote it, and label it as advertising. Fit: High for health, education, real estate and solar/home-improvement lead gen, aimed at cold, problem-aware traffic.

19. **Carousel.** Card 1 is the hook, cards 2–4 the benefits, steps or proof, and the last card the offer and CTA. Lead-gen uses: property galleries, course modules, service menus, case-study steps.

20. **Feature-annotation / benefit-callout static.** The product or service image in the center, 3–5 arrows or labels pointing out benefits, the headline on top and the CTA at the bottom. Lead-gen uses: annotated property photos, clinic equipment, software screens.

21. **Review/star static and social-proof wall.** A grid of 6–12 real review screenshots plus a line like "4.9★ from 1,200+ patients". Strongest for local services that can use Google Maps reviews.

22. **Statistics / press / "as seen in".** One big number ("12,000 families helped"). Only use press logos that are genuine.

23. **Educational / myth-vs-fact / mistakes-to-avoid / FAQ-objection.** A two-panel "Myth / Fact" static, a "3 mistakes" video, or a creator answering the top three objections (price, time, trust). These fit considered purchases well (education, real estate, B2B, health). That matches Motion's "credibility-forward and explanatory" finding for professional services, but there's no direct data on these formats.

**C. Unverified archetypes (templates only; test before investing)**

24. **Expert/authority.** A credential lower-third, a professional setting, and an explainer or myth-bust. Probably strong for health and professional services, based on indirect support from Motion's vertical data.
25. **Case study.** "How [client type] got [result] in [time]": before, what we did, the result, then the CTA, as a carousel or a 30–45 s video. B2B High. Motion shows it is not near the top in fashion.
26. **Behind the scenes.** The team, the process, quality control. Foreplay has a BTS award category, but I found no performance data.
27. **POV / day in the life / ASMR / split-screen.**
    - POV: a text hook such as "POV: you finally found a dentist who…".
    - Day in the life: of a student, an agent or a technician.
    - ASMR: for tactile products and services (cleaning, detailing, spa).
    - Split-screen: process on one side, result on the other.
    - I found no 2025–26 data for any of these.

**Quick map by business type (inference):**
- **Ecommerce:** offer-first, unboxing, demo, UGC and multi-UGC, us-vs-them, carousel, stitch/reaction.
- **Local services:** founder/owner, review wall, before/after (outside health), demo/process, offer-first ("free quote"), green screen over Google reviews.
- **Health/aesthetics:** stitch/reaction by a doctor, founder/doctor, policy-safe testimonials, educational and myth formats. Avoid personal-attribute framing and check the before/after rules.
- **Education:** offer-first with a deadline, student testimonials, day-in-the-life, an unboxing of course materials, a quiz feeding a lead form.
- **Real estate:** demo/tour, street interview, carousel gallery, us-vs-them (buying direct from the developer), advertorials for investors.
- **B2B/professional:** founder or expert talking head, podcast clip, case study, screen-recorded demo, ugly/native text statics.

## 3. How has Andromeda changed creative strategy (diversity vs iteration, near-duplicate grouping, number of concepts, format mix, frameworks)?

### Takeaway
Andromeda, announced Dec 2, 2024, is Meta's ad-retrieval engine, and with it Meta has reframed creative diversification as "the new targeting". By late 2026 that guidance has become concrete in two ways:
- Ads Manager has a "Creative diversity" rating (Low/Medium/High). It rolled out around Aug 26, 2026 and is labeled "in development".
- Agencies told Marketing Brew (Sept 28, 2026) that Meta treats ads with the same look or the same opening hook as essentially the same ad, and ranks them lower.

Meta publishes no official number of creatives. Practitioner ranges cluster around a minimum of 6–10 genuinely distinct concepts per ad set, with 10–20 common. The dominant way to diversify is a matrix of persona × desire/pain × awareness × format, such as P.D.A.

### Cited Findings
**What Andromeda is [META]**
- Meta Engineering, Dec 2, 2024: Andromeda is the machine-learning system for the retrieval stage of ad recommendation. That is the first stage, which narrows tens of millions of candidate ads to a few thousand before ranking. It runs on NVIDIA Grace Hopper and Meta's own MTIA chips. Summaries of the post report +6% recall, +8% ads quality on selected segments, and a 10,000× increase in model complexity. Source: [Meta Engineering](https://engineering.fb.com/2024/12/02/production-engineering/meta-andromeda-advantage-automation-next-gen-personalized-ads-retrieval-engine/)
- Practitioner explainers separate three things: retrieval (Andromeda), ranking (GEM), and Advantage+ (the campaign-automation product). †Sources: [BlckAlpaca](https://blckalpaca.at/en/knowledge-base/social-media/paid-social-performance-marketing/meta-andromeda-gem-advantage-plus.md); [Darkroom](https://www.darkroomagency.com/observatory/meta-andromeda-explained-how-darkroom-leverages-the-new-ad-algorithm)
- The claim that creative is now the main targeting signal comes from agency interpretations, not from Meta's engineering text. (Search finding about the sources above.)

**What Meta says about diversification [META]**

Source: [Jon Loomer, "Creative Diversification Is the New Targeting" (Sept 3, 2025)](https://pubcast.jonloomer.com/creative-diversification-is-the-new-targeting/)

### Inferences
- In late 2026, a "new concept" in practice means a different persona or desire **and** a different look or opening (messenger, setting, format). Changing only the text overlay, headline, color or music is iteration, and probably collapses into the same ad (Cripps, via Marketing Brew).
- Suggested launch pack for a Superleads lead-gen ad set (my inference, within the third-party ranges):
  - Start with 6–10 distinct concepts.
  - Cover at least 3 formats: native static, talking-head video, and demo/process or carousel.
  - Use at least 3 messengers: owner or expert, a real client, a creator or partner.
  - Only then add iterations, and only on concepts that earn spend.
  - With small budgets, run fewer ad sets with more distinct concepts rather than more near-duplicates. Motion shows only about 4% winners at the smallest accounts.
- Lead-gen concept matrix: Persona (2–3 buyer types) × Pain/Desire (2–3) × Awareness (problem-aware / solution-aware / ready to buy) × Messenger × Format. Three examples for a dental clinic:
  - Busy mom, fear of the price, problem-aware, the owner-dentist as messenger, a stitch/reaction video.
  - Young professional, wants better aesthetics, solution-aware, a patient as messenger, a UGC testimonial.
  - Senior, needs to trust an implant clinic, ready to buy, a review-wall static with an offer-first frame.
- Treat the Creative Diversity column as a rough smoke alarm, not a KPI. It is "in development" and reportedly noisy. How spend spreads across your concepts is the more reliable signal.

## 5. Technical specs and best practices (aspect ratios, safe zones, captions, sound-off, length, text on statics) and Partnership ads

### Takeaway
Current practice, as reported by third-party guides:
- **Reels/Stories:** 9:16 masters at 1080×1920 or 1440×2560. Keep text and logos out of the top 14%, the bottom 35% and 6% on each side. Several sources say Meta unified the safe zone across placements in March 2026.
- **Feed:** 4:5 at 1440×1800.
- **Video:** burn in captions, land the hook in the first 1–2 seconds, and keep most videos to 6–30 seconds. There is no single official length.

Partnership ads, which run from a creator's handle, carry Meta-published lifts of 19% lower CPA and 13% higher CTR when added to existing campaigns. Meta is merging its creator tools into a Creator Marketing Hub in 2026.
