## 1. Hooks: which hook formulas and categories do respected practitioners teach (verbal, on-screen text, visual), and which are reported to perform?

### Takeaway
The only large-sample, current performance data on hook types comes from **Motion's Creative Benchmarks 2026** (about $1.29B in Meta spend, Sept 2025 to early Jan 2026). Among the five "hook headline" tactics it tracks, **Confession** has the highest hit rate (8.74%), followed by Curiosity (7.77%), Bold claim (7.19%), Relatability (6.85%) and Contrast (6.78%). Promotional tactics (Newness, Sale Announcement, Price Anchor, Urgency) lead the overall leaderboard. The practitioner frameworks are Hormozi's callouts plus his 7 "headline components" and hook-retain-reward, Savannah Sanchez's fill-in-the-blank UGC hooks and Post-it/visual hooks, Dara Denney's curiosity-gap and cognitive-dissonance approach plus "hook compilations", and Barry Hott's "ugly ads". All of them were reachable only through secondary summaries, so verbatim template wording is thin.

### Cited Findings

#### Data-backed hook performance: Motion Creative Benchmarks 2026
- **Dataset:** $1.29B in realized Meta ad spend across 578,750 creatives and 6,015 advertiser accounts. Elsewhere Motion describes it as "+550,000 ads launched by +6,000 advertisers", about $1.3B across Facebook and Instagram from September 2025 to early January 2026. — [Motion methodology](https://motionapp.com/library/research/creative-benchmarks-2026/methodology); [Motion Creative Benchmarks 2026](https://motionapp.com/thumbstop-pulse/creative-benchmarks-2026/)
- **Hit rate by "hook headline tactic"** (table headed "Hook headline tactics | Winners | Mid-range | Hit rate (%)"): Confession 8.74%, Curiosity 7.77%, Bold claim 7.19%, Relatability 6.85%, Contrast 6.78%. The search tool noted the table text was partly garbled in its extract, so verify before quoting exact decimals. — [Motion: Top hook tactics on Meta in 2026](https://motionapp.com/library/research/creative-benchmarks-2026/top-hook-tactics)
- Motion's overall hit-rate leaders are **promotional** tactics: "Newness, Sale Announcement, Price Anchor, Urgency". "Giveaway and Confession over-index on spend use despite rarer winners." — [Motion: Top hook tactics](https://motionapp.com/library/research/creative-benchmarks-2026/top-hook-tactics)
- Motion's 2026 bootcamp lists the five hook formats as **confession, bold claim, relatability, contrast, curiosity**. Motion says curiosity, confessional framing, bold claims or unexpected statements "interrupt scrolling and create tension or surprise". — [Motion Bootcamp 2026: Top Hook Formats](https://motionapp.com/events/2026-creative-strategy-bootcamp/homebase/top-hook-formats); [Motion glossary: Curiosity hook](https://motionapp.com/library/hooks/tactics/curiosity)
- **Conflicting view:** GetHookd (2026) reports that "confessional, direct address, and contrast hooks are outperforming traditional bold claim hooks as audiences grow more skeptical of over-produced ads". Motion's data ranks bold claim above relatability and contrast. — [GetHookd: 8 Best Facebook Ad Hook Examples for 2026](https://www.gethookd.ai/learn/8-best-facebook-ad-hook-examples-for-2026/); contradicted in part by [Motion](https://motionapp.com/library/research/creative-benchmarks-2026/top-hook-tactics)
- **How Motion defines a "winner" and "hit rate":** a winner spends at least 10× the account's median creative spend and at least $500. Hit rate is computed per account and then averaged within a spend tier (details in Q4). — [Motion methodology](https://motionapp.com/library/research/creative-benchmarks-2026/methodology)

#### Alex Hormozi ($100M Leads, 2023; $100M Playbook: GOATed Ads, Aug 2025)
- *$100M Leads* teaches a **"hook-retain-reward"** system for content, alongside a "six-part ad framework" (per a book summary; the six parts were not retrieved). — [Medium summary of $100M Leads](https://medium.com/@loreviglietti/100m-leads-how-to-get-strangers-to-want-to-buy-your-stuff-by-alex-hormozi-75a733dbe8ce)
- **Seven "headline components"** ("model the news"): **recency, relevancy, celebrity, proximity, conflict, unusual, ongoing.** Examples from summaries:
  - Proximity: "namedrop Madrid, not Mogadishu" for a Spanish audience.
  - Conflict: "Post 2x week vs 7x week?" and "Pineapple vs no Pineapple on pizza? Conflict!"
  - Ongoing: stories still happening, "dynamic", with "plot twists".
  - One LinkedIn summarizer (Aug 29, 2023) says headlines with at least 2 of these concepts "are a hit". That is the author's reading, not confirmed book text.
  - Sources: [Max Mednik notes on $100M Leads](https://www.maxmednik.com/blog/notes-on-100m-leads-by-alex-hormozi); [Salin Awad, LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/salin-awad-6b5257173_alex-hormozis-has-fantastic-advice-for-writing-activity-7102217848017563648-Ij-c); [Quintsmart summary](https://garden.quintsmart.com/comprehensive-summary-based-on-my-highlights)
- **Ad callouts:** one summary gives the ad-side rule as "Call Outs: Get attention through labels, yes-questions, if-then statements, and contrasts". #### Dara Denney (Chief Evangelist at Motion; ex-Thesis, "$100M+" in ad spend managed)
- She treats hook writing as a **psychology problem** rather than a formula. Her levers are a **curiosity gap** or a **conflicting statement that creates cognitive dissonance**, so the viewer stays to resolve it. She studies YouTubers because YouTube is opt-in and creators have to earn the click. — [Motion expert profile](https://motionapp.com/library/expert/dara-denney/); [Geoly profile](https://www.geoly.ai/blog/geo-expert-dara-denney-dtc-ad-creative) (secondary)
- **Named hooks from a write-up of her "1,000+ ads tested" video** (secondary):
  - The **"Scam Hook"**: the word "scam" triggers loss aversion and curiosity.

  - Viewers relate to shared experiences and previous failures.

#### Visual hooks and on-screen text rules
- **Reels safe zones:**
  - Keep text and logos out of roughly the **top 14%**, **bottom 35%** and **6% on each side** of a 9:16 frame. — [House of Marketers safe-zone guide](https://www.houseofmarketers.com/guide-to-safe-zones-tiktok-facebook-instagram-stories); [Strike Social templates](https://strikesocial.com/?p=264470)
  - On a 1440×2560 canvas that is about 358px top, 896px bottom and 86px per side. — [Moda: Meta Reels ad](https://moda.app/resources/sizes/meta-reels-ad)
  - Billo says Meta **unified the 9:16 safe zone for Stories and Reels in March 2026** and gives the bottom margin as a 20–35% range. Conservative teams still treat the full 35% as off-limits because captions expand. Single source, so verify. — [Billo: Meta ads safe zones 2026](https://billo.app/blog/meta-ads-safe-zones)
  - Build to 10% per side to survive edge trimming on tall screens. — [Adnova](https://adnova.ai/blogs/meta-ad-safe-zones-guide)
- **Don't burn the CTA into the video or image.** Use Meta's CTA button, which would overlap a burned-in CTA. — [Moda](https://moda.app/resources/sizes/meta-reels-ad)
- **Sound-off design:** the consensus is to design for sound-off with captions and on-screen text, then add audio. One source says Meta does not auto-caption Facebook Reels ads, so burn captions in. Older stats in these guides ("up to 85% of Facebook video is watched without sound"; "65% of viewers who watch the first 3 seconds will continue watching for ten seconds or more") are unattributed there and probably date from about 2016. — [GetHookd: Reels ads best practices](https://www.gethookd.ai/learn/facebook-reels-ads-specs-examples-best-practices/); [Benly: creative best practices](https://benly.ai/learn/meta-ads/creative-best-practices) (claims not mapped to individual pages)
- **A hook must stop the scroll visually before the audio registers.** Build natively in 9:16, because cropped horizontal or square video "almost always hurts performance". — [GetHookd](https://www.gethookd.ai/learn/facebook-reels-ads-specs-examples-best-practices/); [Benly Reels guide](https://benly.ai/learn/meta-ads/meta-ads-reels-ads-guide)

## 2. Copy: primary-text and headline frameworks, length and formatting guidance, and copy as a targeting signal after Andromeda

### Takeaway
I could verify Meta field-length conventions (about 125 visible characters of primary text, headlines around 27–40 characters), Hormozi's callout → value (value equation) → CTA structure, and how Andromeda changed copy's role. Copy is now part of the creative Andromeda reads to find the audience, so the advice is to **vary the angle or value proposition, not cosmetic wording**, and to name the audience explicitly. I could not retrieve 2025-2026 practitioner sources on classic frameworks (PAS, AIDA, BAB, 4Ps, PASTOR, Hook-Story-Offer, 4 U's, Schwartz) or on short vs. long copy and formatting. Those are gaps below.

### Cited Findings

#### Meta copy fields: length guidance
- **Primary text:**
  - About **125 characters** are visible before "See more" truncation. Meta "recommend[s] 125 characters for most placements", as quoted by one source.
  - Up to **five primary text options** per ad.
  - Sources: [Influee: Facebook ad copy examples 2026](https://influee.co/blog/facebook-ad-copy); [SaveMyLeads: Meta ads character limits](https://savemyleads.com/blog/other/meta-ads-character-limits); [AdsUploader: Meta ad copy specs](https://adsuploader.com/blog/meta-ad-copy-specs)
- **Headline:**
  - About **40 characters** for most placements, but **27 for Facebook Feed**.
  - The Reels overlay headline can drop to **10**.
  - Same sources as primary text.
- **Description:** sources conflict: **25, 27 or 30 characters**. — [SaveMyLeads](https://savemyleads.com/blog/other/meta-ads-character-limits); [AdsUploader](https://adsuploader.com/blog/meta-ad-copy-specs); [Influee](https://influee.co/blog/facebook-ad-copy)
- **Reels-specific limits (Moda):** 44 characters of primary text for Instagram Reels, 40 for Facebook Reels, and a 55-character headline. The headline figure conflicts with the 40 and 27 above. — [Moda: Meta Reels ad](https://moda.app/resources/sizes/meta-reels-ad)

#### Structures with practitioner attribution
- **Hormozi ad anatomy** (*$100M Leads*, per summaries):
  1. **Callout:** labels, yes-questions, if-then statements, contrasts.
  2. **Value:** built on his **value equation** (dream outcome, perceived likelihood of achievement, time delay, effort and sacrifice).
  3. **CTA:** tell the audience exactly what to do next, and keep the landing page simple and matched to the ad.
  - Sources: [Business Book Club](https://businessbookclub.substack.com/p/free-book-summary-100m-leads-by-alex); [Max Mednik](https://www.maxmednik.com/blog/notes-on-100m-leads-by-alex-hormozi)
- **GOATed Ads (Aug 2025):** hooks / meat / CTA, many variations, and a CTA chapter on adding urgency. — [Google Books](https://books.google.com/books/about/100M_Playbook_GOATed_Ads.html?id=Uq520QEACAAJ); [Scribd](https://www.scribd.com/document/906072545/100M-Goated-Ads-Playbook)
- **Hormozi "Proof–Promise–Plan"** (a third-party interpretation of his pitch structure):
  - The plan answers how the offer works, because prospects hesitate when they can't picture the process.
  - Proof means testimonials, case studies, guarantees and social proof.
  - The source suggests 1–2 minutes of proof, 2–3 of promise and 1 of plan in a five-minute pitch. That split is the third party's.
  - Source: [AmpiFire](https://ampifire.com/blog/alex-hormozi-proof-promise-plan-framework-to-sell-your-idea/)

This is a real hypothesis I recently wrote for a brand: Many of BRAND's customers are problem-aware, but not really 'solution' aware. DEMOGRAPHIC seem to be aware of their PROBLEM, but they are only aware of COMMON SOLUTION. Fo…" (truncated). This applies Schwartz-style awareness levels to a test hypothesis. — [Dara Denney on X](https://twitter.com/DenneyDara/status/1760358318294331837)

#### Copy as a targeting signal after Andromeda
- Meta's April 2025 blog, as quoted by Jon Loomer, makes **creative diversification** the main lever for finding relevant audiences, with Andromeda choosing which creative to show. — [Jon Loomer: Meta Andromeda](https://www.jonloomer.com/meta-andromeda/)

- A cited "60% similarity threshold" is unverified. So are vendor claims like "10,000x efficiency" and "65% higher ROAS". — [ChatterBuzz](https://www.chatterbuzzmedia.com/blog/meta-andromeda-creative-targeting/)

### Inferences
- **Primary-text template**, Hormozi callout → value → CTA (my wording; structure sourced):
  1. **Line 1 (fits in the 125 visible characters):** callout plus outcome. "[City] [avatar]: [dream outcome] in [time] — without [effort/sacrifice]."
  2. **Lines 2–4, proof (likelihood):** "[N] [clients/patients] in [area] already [result]." Or a short testimonial line.
  3. **Lines 5–6, how it works (lowers effort and time delay):** "1) [step] 2) [step] 3) [step]."
  4. **Qualifier, for lead gen:** "This is for [who]. Not for [who]." This helps with lead quality, and under Andromeda it also tells the system who the ad is for.
  5. **CTA, with urgency per GOATed Ads:** "Tap [Get quote/Send WhatsApp] — [N] spots this week."
- **Headline:** 27–40 characters, carrying the *offer or value proposition*, not a restated hook. Make each headline variant a different value proposition (Andromeda guidance), e.g. "Free 15-min assessment", "Quote in 24h", "Pay in 12 months".
- **Awareness-based angle planning** (Denney's hypothesis pattern): write one body per awareness stage. Problem-aware: name the pain. Solution-aware: contrast with the common solution. Product-aware: proof plus offer. These are distinct concepts, not 3-2-2 cosmetic variants.
- **Under Andromeda, copy variety is diversification only when the angle differs.** Ten rephrasings of one promise likely count as one creative.

### Gaps
- **Classic framework definitions (not verified this session; standard copywriting canon; confirm before publishing):**
  - **PAS:** Problem–Agitate–Solution.
  - **AIDA:** Attention–Interest–Desire–Action, traditionally credited to E. St. Elmo Lewis.
  - **BAB:** Before–After–Bridge.
  - **4Ps:** Picture–Promise–Prove–Push, usually credited to Henry Hoke.
  - **PASTOR:** Problem, Amplify, Story/Solution, Transformation/Testimony, Offer, Response (Ray Edwards).
  - **Hook–Story–Offer:** Russell Brunson.
  - **4 U's:** Urgent, Unique, Ultra-specific, Useful (Michael Masterson/AWAI).
  - **Schwartz's five awareness levels** (*Breakthrough Advertising*, 1966): Unaware, Problem-aware, Solution-aware, Product-aware, Most aware.

## 3. Scripts: video script templates with timing (UGC 15/30/60s, founder, testimonial, demo, problem-solution, long-form/VSL), modular scripting, and creator briefs

### Takeaway
The sourced consensus skeleton is **hook 0–3s → problem (about 3–8s) → solution or demo (about 8–20s) → CTA in the final 3–5s**. 15s compresses the middle, 30s is the "sweet spot" with room for 2–3 proof points, and 60s becomes a mini-story. These timings come from vendor UGC guides, not named top practitioners. Practitioners agree on **modular production**: several hooks over one body (Sanchez split-tests hooks over an identical body; Hormozi's hooks/meat/CTA variations; Denney's "hook compilation"). Briefs should be 1–2 pages, give 3–5 hook options to paraphrase, and ask for alternative hooks and a swappable CTA as deliverables.

### Cited Findings

#### Timing skeletons (vendor UGC guides; claims not mapped to individual pages)
- **Hook at 0–3s:** "you have roughly three seconds to earn the next thirty".
- **15s:** four 3–5s blocks (attention, interest, desire, action). "If the hook is weak, 15 seconds isn't enough to recover."
- **30s:** called the sweet spot, with room for 2–3 proof points instead of one.
- **60s:** a mini-story. One guide combines a PAS opening, BAB body and AIDA close.
- **Common skeleton:** hook 0–3s, problem 3–8s, solution 8–20s, CTA in the final 3–5s. 15s compresses the middle, 30s balances the parts, 60s expands the demo.

## 4. Creative-testing methodology: concepts vs. iterations, 3-2-2, cadence and volume by spend, budgets, duration, statistical caveats, kill rules, iteration playbooks, naming

### Takeaway
Winners are rare and spend-defined. In Motion's 2026 data only about **4–8% of creatives become winners** (≥10× the account's median creative spend and ≥$500), and winners absorb about **55% of spend**. Volume scales with spend tier: an average of **2.8 new creatives a week under $10K a month, up to 18.9 at $1M+**, with top-quartile accounts shipping roughly 2–3× more. Practitioner rules of thumb range from Denney's **"for every $1000/day in spend... at least 1 creative test per week"** to Foxwell's concurrency guidance (about 15 ads per $100/day ad set). The common kill rule is **zero conversions after 2–3× target CPA** with a spend floor. Judge attention metrics early and CPA later. Meta's native **Creative Testing** tool (2–5 variants, possibly 10; ≤20% of budget suggested) is the cleanest way to run a controlled comparison.

### Cited Findings

#### What counts as a winner, and how rare winners are (Motion 2026)
- **Definitions:**
  - **Winner:** spend ≥10× the account's median creative spend **and** ≥$500.
  - **Mid-range:** spent for ≥28 days in the window but not a winner.
  - **Hit rate:** computed per account, then averaged within a tier with accounts weighted equally.
  - **Top accounts:** the top 25% by winner count in each tier. Comparisons never cross tiers.
  - **Spend, not ROAS or CPA,** is the success metric, because spend is comparable across 6,000+ accounts with different business models and measurement setups.
  - Source: [Motion methodology](https://motionapp.com/library/research/creative-benchmarks-2026/methodology); [Motion: the 10× threshold](https://motionapp.com/library/research/creative-benchmarks-2026/10x-benchmark)
- **Hit rate by tier:** Micro (<$10K/mo) 3.7%, Small 6.2%, Medium 7.3%, Large 8.1%, Enterprise ($1M+/mo) 8.2%. A FAQ version gives about 3.8% for Micro and 8.2% for Enterprise. Adliftr attributes about 4% and about 9% to Motion, which conflicts slightly. — [Motion talk: Meta Ads in 2026: How Many Creatives Do You Need](https://motionapp.com/library/talk/meta-ads-in-2026-how-many-creatives-do-you-actually-need-to-launch/); [Motion: winners are rare](https://motionapp.com/library/research/creative-benchmarks-2026/winners-are-rare); [Adliftr](https://adliftr.com/blog/ad-creative-testing-statistics-2026)
- "Only 5–8% of ads become real winners", and about half never get meaningful spend. — [Motion Bootcamp: 2026 benchmarks](https://motionapp.com/events/2026-creative-strategy-bootcamp/homebase/2026-creative-benchmarks)
- **About 55% of Meta spend goes to winners**, and the share rises with advertiser size. — [Motion: spend concentration](https://motionapp.com/library/research/creative-benchmarks-2026/spend-concentration)

#### Test structure
- **Denney's structure (Feb 2024, pre-Andromeda):**
  - Run a separate creative-testing campaign, with each new test in a new ad set of **2–4 variations** on broad targeting.
  - Brands now take "bigger swings", changing messaging and imagery together.
  - True A/B testing and statistical significance aren't required for actionable insights.
  - Write a hypothesis for every test.
  - Rank candidate tests by performance potential.
  - Sources: [Dara Denney LinkedIn: How to Test Facebook Ads Creative in 2024](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/daradenney_how-to-test-facebook-ads-creative-in-2024-activity-7166149718102757378-Rc2E); [Dara Denney on X](https://twitter.com/DenneyDara/status/1760358318294331837)
- **Meta's native Creative Testing tool:**
  - **Setup:** open the edit view of an existing ad, go to "Creative Testing", then "Set up test".
  - **Variants:** create **2–5 new variants**. The original ad isn't part of the test, and you can't test existing ads, only new duplicates.
  - Meta's wording ("up to five versions") could mean six ads. Many advertisers now see a limit of **10**, but this varies by account.
  - **Budget:** one guide says Meta recommends **no more than 20% of budget** for the test.
  - **Duration:** sources disagree: a 7-day default vs. up to 30 days.
  - **Metrics:** CPC, CPM, or cost per any standard event, custom event or custom conversion.
  - Sources: [Jon Loomer: Meta's Creative Testing Tool](https://www.jonloomer.com/meta-creative-testing/); [Jon Loomer: expanding to 10 ads](https://www.jonloomer.com/qvt/meta-expanding-creative-testing-10-ads/); [FirstPier: Meta creative testing quick start](https://www.firstpier.com/resources/meta-ads-creative-testing); [Jon Loomer pubcast, Jan 6, 2026](https://pubcast.jonloomer.com/make-creative-testing-part-of-your-process/)
- **Test size, and a conflict with 3-2-2:**
  - FirstPier limits a single test to **3–5 creatives** so budget isn't fragmented. It notes that Meta's algorithm-driven dynamic tests (such as 3-2-2) don't isolate variables: you learn *which* combination won, not *why*. — [FirstPier](https://www.firstpier.com/resources/facebook-ads-creative-testing)
  - 3-2-2 deliberately creates 12 combinations. — [Madgicx](https://madgicx.com/blog/facebook-ad-copy)

#### Concept vs. iteration
- **Denney:** "iterations versus net-new creative" is a core prioritization decision (Feb 2024). A summary of a more recent video says: **if one ad is carrying the account, iterate first; if performance is diversified, push harder on net-new** (aggregator, secondhand). — [Dara Denney LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/daradenney_how-to-test-facebook-ads-creative-in-2024-activity-7166149718102757378-Rc2E); [TikTok aggregator](https://www.tiktok.com/discover/creative-testing-framework)
- **Iteration dimensions:** Denney's analysis axes are format, creator, messaging, imagery and persona, and she weights the creator heavily (Dec 2025). — [Motion](https://motionapp.com/library/talk/dara-denney-reveals-the-biggest-creative-analysis-mistake-in-2026-most/)
- **Andromeda:** near-duplicate variants get grouped, so cosmetic iterations add little reach. Diversify concepts. — [Olbuz](https://www.olbuz.com/blog/meta-andromeda-ai-audience-targeting); [Jon Loomer](https://www.jonloomer.com/meta-andromeda/)

#### Decision and kill rules (agency and tool-vendor heuristics; no official Meta source)
- **Common rule:** kill a variant with **zero conversions after spending 2–3× target CPA**. Some frameworks use 3–5× target cost per purchase.
- **Confidence-based version:** one kill table gives about **2.3× target CPA** (aggressive) or about **3.0×** (conservative) at zero conversions.
- **Floors and timing:** give each test 2–3× target CPA with a floor of about **$100–150** before judging on purchases. Wait 3–5 days before optimizing, and don't kill on CPA in the first 48 hours.
- **Kill early on attention** (hook rate, CTR) and **late on CPA**.
- **CBO caveat:** the rule matters most under CBO, where Meta may dump budget into one variant on early click signals.
- The search summary blended these guides without mapping each number to one page.
- Sources: [AdManage: When to kill a Facebook ad](https://admanage.ai/blog/when-to-kill-a-facebook-ad); [Rule1: creative testing framework](https://rule1.ai/articles/creative-testing-framework); [SparkUGC: how much to spend per creative](https://www.sparkugc.com/resources/how-much-to-spend-per-creative-meta-ads-test); [Adens Lab: budget split](https://www.adenslab.com/blog/best-budget-split-meta-ad-creative-testing); [Young Urban Project: decision framework](https://www.youngurbanproject.com/how-to-build-a-meta-ads-creative-decision-framework/); [AnyTrack: automation rules](https://anytrack.io/blog/meta-ads-automation-rules-that-actually-work-in-2025)
- **Hook-only tests:** read after 48–72 hours. — [Scrolling Media](https://www.scrolling.media/post/stop-testing-ads-start-testing-hooks)
- **Statistical caveats:**
  - Barry Hott: higher hook rate or CTR doesn't mean better performance. — [Sepia Lab](https://sepia-lab.com/en/blog/hook-rate-benchmarks)
  - Motion: volume correlates with winners but isn't proven causal; spend is the comparable success metric. — [Motion methodology](https://motionapp.com/library/research/creative-benchmarks-2026/methodology)
  - Denney: statistical significance isn't required for actionable insights (2024). — [LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/daradenney_how-to-test-facebook-ads-creative-in-2024-activity-7166149718102757378-Rc2E)

### Inferences
- **Suggested cadence for a lead-gen account.** Anchor on Motion's averages, apply Denney's $1K/day rule, and cap concurrent ads per ad set with Foxwell's concurrency guidance:

  | Monthly spend | New concepts per week | Hook/format variants per week | Notes |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | <$10K | 1–3 (Motion avg 2.8) | 2–3 | A $100/day ad set can hold about 15 ads (Foxwell) |
  | $10–50K | 1–2 tests of 2–4 creatives (Denney: about 1 test per $1K/day) | Total about 4–8 creatives (Motion all 4.1, top 25% 8.1) | — |
  | $50–200K | 2–4 tests | About 7–16 creatives (Motion 6.7 / 16.0) | — |

  US tiers will not map one-to-one to Mexico, because CPMs and CPLs differ. See Q5 gaps.
- **Decision tree:**
  1. **Day 2–3:** check attention. A hook rate under about 20–25% suggests the opening is failing, so swap hooks and keep the body.
  2. **At 1× target CPL with zero leads:** keep the ad if CTR and hook are healthy; otherwise turn it off.
  3. **At 2–3× target CPL with zero leads:** turn it off.
  4. **Graduate to the scaling campaign** when CPL is at or under target over enough leads.
  5. **Lead gen tie-breaker:** lead quality from the CRM (contact rate, appointment rate), not just CPL.
- **Iteration playbook for a winner** (from Denney's dimensions and Sanchez's hook split-test):
  1. New hooks over the same body (3–5).
  2. A new messenger or creator with the same script.
  3. A new format (static, carousel or UGC version of the same angle).
  4. A new persona or awareness-stage angle (a net-new concept).
  - Under Andromeda, steps 3–4 count as diversification; steps 1–2 refresh fatigue.
- **Naming convention** (synthesized, mirroring Denney's analysis axes so ads can be tagged and analyzed later):
  - Pattern: `YYMMDD_ConceptID_Angle_Awareness_Persona_Format_Creator_HookID_Length_CTA_v#`
  - Example: `261011_C014_PrecioAncla_SolAware_DuenoRestaurante_UGC_MariaG_H3_30s_WA_v2`
- **Spend-based winners for lead gen:** using Motion's spend-based winner definition (≥10× account median spend) is a pragmatic way to spot winners even when downstream lead quality data lags.

## 5. Metrics and benchmarks: definitions, formula variants, and what "good" looks like in 2025-2026 (lead gen vs. ecommerce)

### Takeaway
**Hook rate (thumbstop) = 3-second video plays ÷ impressions** is near-universal. "Good" is roughly **25–30%+**: Motion's 2025 bands call under 25% "needs work" and 25–35% "solid", and one dataset puts the Meta median at 28% Feed and 31% Reels. **Hold rate has competing formulas**: 15-second plays ÷ 3-second plays (Motion blog), ThruPlays ÷ 3-second plays (Motion glossary and most guides), or ÷ impressions (some teams), so benchmarks range from about 12–25% to 40–60% depending on the denominator. **WordStream/LocaliQ (US):**
- **2025 lead campaigns:** CTR 2.59%, CVR 7.72%, CPL $27.66 (+~21% YoY).
- **2026 edition:** CVR 8.54%, CPL $27.39.
- **2025 traffic campaigns:** CTR 1.71%, CPC $0.70.

Triple Whale (mostly ecommerce, 2025) shows a median CTR of 2.19% and a CPM of about $13.5–14.2.

### Cited Findings

#### Definitions and formula variants
- **Hook rate / thumbstop** = 3-second video plays (views) ÷ impressions. — [Motion Creative Benchmarks 2026 (search summary)](https://motionapp.com/thumbstop-pulse/creative-benchmarks-2026/); [AdMake AI: hook rate](https://admakeai.com/blog/hook-rate)
- **Hold rate, variant A (Motion blog):** 15-second video plays ÷ 3-second video plays × 100. — [Motion: key creative performance metrics](https://motionapp.com/blog/key-creative-performance-metrics)
- **Hold rate, variant B (Motion glossary, Attribuly, and "most sources"):** "(Through plays ÷ 3-Second Video Plays) × 100 = Hold rate". — [Motion glossary: Hold rate](https://library.motionapp.com/glossary/hold-rate); [Attribuly](https://attribuly.com/blogs/facebook-ads-hold-rate-shopify-benchmarks/)
- **Hold rate, variant C (impressions denominator):** some teams divide by impressions, which gives much smaller numbers. "A 12% figure off impressions and a 12% figure off 3-second views are not comparable." Hold rate is **not an official Meta metric**. — [Jetfuel: hold rate, thumbstop and ThruPlay benchmarks 2026](https://jetfuel.agency/creative-performance-metrics-for-dtc-video-ads-hold-rate-thumbstop-and-thruplay-benchmarks-2026/)
- **ThruPlay (Meta, as cited):** counted when a video under 15s plays to completion, or at 15 seconds for longer videos. — [Jetfuel](https://jetfuel.agency/creative-performance-metrics-for-dtc-video-ads-hold-rate-thumbstop-and-thruplay-benchmarks-2026/)
- **Click types:**
  - **Clicks (all)** includes reactions, comments and shares.
  - **Link clicks** are clicks to advertiser-chosen destinations on or off Meta, including click-to-call, click-to-message, lead forms and playables.
  - **Outbound clicks** only count clicks that leave Meta's apps, including links in Instant Experiences, lead forms and collections.
  - **CTR (all)** = clicks (all) ÷ impressions. **CTR (link)** = link clicks ÷ impressions. **Outbound CTR** = outbound clicks ÷ impressions.
  - One commentator says **unique outbound CTR** most accurately measures clicks to your website.
  - Sources: [Motion glossary: Click](https://motionapp.com/library/glossary/click); [Jon Loomer: outbound clicks](https://www.jonloomer.com/how-meta-could-leverage-outbound-clicks/); [AdsUploader: Facebook ads CTR](https://adsuploader.com/blog/facebook-ads-ctr)
- **Motion "winner" and "hit rate"** (spend-based) are defined in Q4. — [Motion methodology](https://motionapp.com/library/research/creative-benchmarks-2026/methodology)

#### Hook rate benchmarks (Meta)
- **Motion's 2025 thresholds** (per Sepia Lab): under 25% "needs work", 25–35% "solid". — [Sepia Lab: hook rate benchmarks 2026](https://sepia-lab.com/en/blog/hook-rate-benchmarks)
- A good Meta hook rate is about **≥30%** and a good hold rate about **25%**, citing Motion's 2026 report. — [Taylor Sicard: Meta ads creative benchmarks 2026](https://taylorsicard.com/blog/meta-ads-creative-benchmarks-2026)
- About **25% is average and ≥30% strong**; under 20% means the first frame gets scrolled past. — [AdMake AI](https://admakeai.com/blog/hook-rate)
- **Median 28% (Feed) and 31% (Reels)** on Meta, and 33% on TikTok (Benly.ai, 12,000+ ads, via Adliftr). — [Adliftr: ad creative testing stats 2026](https://adliftr.com/blog/ad-creative-testing-statistics-2026)

- **Lead gen vs. ecommerce:**
  - Lead campaigns show *higher* CTR (2.59%) but *higher* CPC ($1.92) than traffic campaigns (1.71%, $0.70). This is consistent with Meta optimizing lead campaigns toward converters rather than cheap clicks.
  - For lead gen the main KPI is CPL plus downstream quality (contact and appointment rates). For ecommerce it's ROAS and CPA (Triple Whale median ROAS 1.86).
  - Instant-form leads count "link clicks" (lead-form opens) that aren't outbound clicks, so **outbound CTR understates engagement for instant-form lead ads**. Use link CTR or form-open rate there. This follows from Meta's click definitions above.
- **Choose one hold-rate formula and document it.** I suggest ThruPlays ÷ 3-second plays, since it is the most common. Report hook rate on impressions, not reach. Many published "benchmarks" are not comparable because of denominators.
